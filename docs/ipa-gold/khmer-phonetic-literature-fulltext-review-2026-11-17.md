# Phnom Penh Khmer phonetic literature — full-text evidence addendum

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** full-text review of Chem (2020) and Mam, Pittayaporn & Maspong (2025), following the completed literature search. This is a source audit, not an approved IPA convention, a lexical pilot manifest, or gold annotation.

## Chem (2020): *The Standard Khmer Vowel System: An Acoustic Study*

DOI: https://doi.org/10.61945/cjbar.2020.2.2.3

### Exact method and locators
- The paper describes an initial observation stage of 21 native Khmer speakers (12 women), 168 lexical items, two repetitions, and formal/casual speech. It was used to select clearer-voiced speakers; see Methods, printed pp. 104–105 (PDF pp. 12–13).
- The final acoustic sample comprises four native Phnom Penh speakers (two women), aged 20–50, with bachelor’s/master’s education and administration, education or media occupations. The author describes selection using clear voice, microphone experience and ability to speak naturally before a microphone; the criteria are not operationalized or independently reproducible.
- The final task included isolated-word reading followed by 61 lexical items read formally three times in a carrier sentence; the paper says these cover 34 vowel-nucleus clusters (21 monophthongs and 13 diphthongs). The complete 61-item list is not published (Methods, printed p. 105 / PDF p. 13).
- The paper reports that unfamiliar items could elicit unnatural stress or a “not a word” response; some gaps were filled using the target vowel in other words/sentences, without a token-level replacement manifest (printed p. 106 / PDF p. 14).
- Acoustic measurements include F1/F2 and duration; the paper discusses coda and syllable-position effects. It does not measure F0 or stress as acoustic outcomes (Methods, printed pp. 105–107 / PDF pp. 13–15).
- The paper reports 10 long + 11 short monophthongs and 10 long + 3 short diphthongs, but its inventory/transcription presentation is internally inconsistent. Mean durations are reported as 76 ms (short monophthongs), 204 ms (long monophthongs), 215 ms (long diphthongs) and 130 ms (short diphthongs) (printed pp. 109–115 / PDF pp. 17–23).
- Table 1 compares authors’ diphthong notation; it is not the elicitation wordlist. Table 4’s F1/F2 grouping labels are unclear. Figures 3–8 show vowel-space, diphthong trajectories and duration summaries; short-diphthong transitions are described as difficult to distinguish (printed pp. 109–113 / PDF pp. 17–21).

### Evidence boundary
This is directly relevant acoustic evidence for a formal-reading Phnom Penh vowel study, but not a reproducible 61-word source list, not a complete IPA convention, and not a broad population sample. The author acknowledges that “Standard Khmer” has no clear-cut definition and that Standard Khmer is not simply identical to Phnom Penh speech (Introduction, printed pp. 95–96 / PDF pp. 3–4). Do not equate “clear speech”, formal reading, Standard Khmer and the project’s careful-citation profile without an expert ruling. Do not derive IPA inventory values from the paper’s summary tables without visual source-page review and specialist reconciliation.

## Mam, Pittayaporn & Maspong (2025): *Phonologization of Pitch in Phnom Penh and Standard Khmer for Cluster and Disyllabic Words*

DOI: https://doi.org/10.21437/TAI.2025-29

### Exact method and locators
- Five native Khmer speakers (two men, three women), aged 20–25, were born, raised and educated for 12 years in Greater Phnom Penh; some lived in Phnom Penh or nearby Ta Khmau. The paper distinguishes Standard Khmer (SK) from Phnom Penh Khmer (PPK), but does not define independent diagnostic criteria for classifying each speaker/token (Methodology §2.1, PDF p. 2).
- The wordlist has 50 targets across cluster and disyllabic structures and four onset categories: r>h, voiced, voiceless stop and nasal. Table 1 provides category examples and glosses, not the complete wordlist.
- Participants saw Khmer orthographic prompts and produced three repetitions in frame sentences, first PPK then SK, with separate frames for the two varieties (Methodology §2.1, PDF p. 2).
- Analysis uses the rime of the stressed syllable. F0 was extracted with CREPE; low-confidence points and outliers were removed; trajectories were time-warped to 51 points, smoothed and speaker-normalized. The first functional principal component accounts for 81% of variance and is interpreted as curve height (§§2.2–3.1, PDF pp. 2–3).

### Findings and evidence boundary
- The study reports higher rime pitch for cluster words than for the second syllable of disyllables, with a larger difference in PPK. Reported effects are in normalized principal-component score units, not Hz or semitones.
- PPK patterns include lower pitch associated with r>h and with voiced/nasal onsets in some disyllabic conditions. Voiced and nasal distributions are broad/bimodal; the authors state that pitch may have phonologized, not that a stable categorical lexical-tone inventory has been demonstrated.
- No perception experiment establishes categorical listener use of a tone contrast. The full 50-word list is not supplied, and the authors note that more words are needed for some onset comparisons (discussion/conclusion, PDF pp. 3–4).

This is bounded evidence for onset- and syllable-conditioned F0 differences in a small Greater Phnom Penh sample. It does not establish a general tone mark, a categorical tone system for every word, or an isolated citation-form rule. Preserve F0 separately if measured; do not add tone diacritics to IPA on this source alone.

## Cross-source decision
1. Chem (2020) can inform a specialist-reviewed vowel/acoustic discussion but cannot supply a reproducible lexical item manifest or a complete transcription convention.
2. Mam et al. (2025) justify keeping pitch/F0 as a research variable and avoiding premature categorical tone encoding; they do not establish a project-wide tone rule.
3. Neither paper supplies the complete source-exact 30 lexeme groups required for the pilot. Their examples and tables must not be silently promoted into gold records.
4. Existing candidate audio datasets (Kirby/DataShare) remain the strongest direct route to inspect actual Phnom Penh recordings, but archive manifests/checksums and target-profile acceptance remain outstanding.
5. The convention draft remains `DRAFT_NOT_APPROVED`; the internal `ipa_kind` remains `UNRESOLVED`; independent adjudicated gold remains 0 and empirical accuracy remains null.
